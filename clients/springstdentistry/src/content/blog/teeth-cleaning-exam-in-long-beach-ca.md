## The importance of regular checkups and cleanings

Teeth cleanings and exams are the foundation of good oral hygiene — and the reason is simple: your toothbrush and floss can only do so much. Plaque, the sticky bacterial film that forms on your teeth every day, hardens into tartar (also called calculus) within 24 to 72 hours of forming. Once it hardens, no amount of brushing at home will remove it. Only a dental hygienist using specialized tools can safely scale it away.

That matters because tartar buildup along the gumline is one of the primary drivers of gum disease. Early-stage gum disease (gingivitis) causes red, puffy gums that bleed when you brush. At that stage, it's fully reversible with a professional cleaning and better home care. Left untreated, it can progress to periodontitis, which involves bone and tissue loss around the teeth — damage that can't be undone, only managed.

Beyond gum disease, regular exams give your dentist a chance to catch cavities while they're small. A cavity treated early typically requires a straightforward filling. A cavity caught late may require a root canal, a crown, or in the worst cases, an extraction. Routine visits are, in a very practical sense, the least expensive dental care you'll ever receive.

## What to expect during a teeth cleaning and exam

Here's a look at what's involved in a typical teeth cleaning and exam, and how it helps keep your smile in great shape:

- **Thorough cleaning:** Your hygienist will remove plaque and tartar from your teeth and around the gumline using handheld scalers or an ultrasonic scaler, then polish the surfaces to remove light staining and give you that freshly cleaned feeling.
- **Gum measurements:** The hygienist may probe around each tooth to measure the depth of the pockets between your teeth and gums. Healthy pockets are 1–3 mm deep. Deeper readings can signal gum disease.
- **Dental exam:** Dr. Hoang will check for signs of cavities, gum disease, and other oral health concerns, including a look at your bite, existing restorations, and soft tissue to ensure that nothing goes unnoticed.
- **X-rays (when needed):** Bitewing or full-mouth X-rays may be taken, typically once a year or based on your individual risk level. These reveal decay between teeth and below the gumline that isn't visible to the naked eye.
- **Personalized advice:** You'll receive recommendations for brushing and flossing based on your specific needs — not generic instructions, but guidance tailored to what your hygienist actually sees in your mouth.
- **Prevention tips:** Dr. Hoang may suggest treatments like fluoride or sealants to further protect your smile against future issues, particularly if certain areas show a higher risk for decay.
- **Comfortable experience:** Most patients find the appointment straightforward. Sensitive teeth or anxiety about dental visits are worth mentioning ahead of time so the team can make adjustments.

The appointment generally wraps up with a summary of findings and, if anything needs follow-up, a clear explanation of what that would involve and why.

## How often do you actually need a cleaning?

The standard recommendation is every six months, and for many patients that's the right interval. But it's worth knowing that this schedule isn't one-size-fits-all.

Patients with a history of gum disease, diabetes, dry mouth, or a tendency to build up tartar quickly may benefit from three- or four-month cleaning cycles. On the other hand, some adults with consistently healthy gums and low decay risk might be fine at an annual interval — though most dentists and hygienists prefer to maintain the six-month schedule because it gives them regular opportunities to catch anything early.

If you're not sure what's right for you, ask at your next appointment. The answer should be based on your specific history, not a default recommendation.

## What happens if you skip cleanings for a long time?

It's common for patients in Long Beach — and everywhere — to fall out of the habit of regular dental visits. Life gets busy, costs add up, or anxiety about the dentist makes it easy to put off. But the longer the gap, the more buildup there tends to be, and the more involved the cleaning appointment becomes.

If it's been two or more years since your last cleaning, the appointment may take longer than a standard hour, and you might need more than one visit to address significant buildup. In some cases, if gum disease has progressed, a standard cleaning isn't the right first step — a deeper cleaning procedure called scaling and root planing may be recommended instead. This goes below the gumline to clean the root surfaces and is usually done in two or more visits with local anesthetic.

None of this is a reason to keep avoiding the dentist. The longer the gap, the more it costs — in time, money, and dental health — to get back on track. Starting again is always the right move, regardless of how long it's been.

## Regular visits support long-term health

Routine cleanings and exams are essential for maintaining your smile and preventing dental issues that compound over time. Teeth aren't the only thing at stake, either. Research has consistently linked poor oral health — particularly gum disease — to systemic conditions including cardiovascular disease, diabetes complications, and adverse pregnancy outcomes. The mechanism isn't fully understood, but the associations are strong enough that many physicians now ask patients about their dental care as part of general health screening.

From a purely practical standpoint, keeping up with regular visits means you're unlikely to face major dental emergencies or expensive restorative procedures unexpectedly. ## Frequently asked questions

**How long does a routine teeth cleaning and exam take?**
A standard cleaning and exam for an adult with reasonably healthy teeth typically takes 45 to 60 minutes. If it's been a long time since your last visit, or if there's significant tartar buildup, the appointment may run longer or require a follow-up visit.

**Does a teeth cleaning hurt?**
For most patients, a cleaning is mildly uncomfortable at worst — some scraping sensations and pressure, particularly around the gumline. If your gums are inflamed or you have sensitive teeth, it can be more uncomfortable. Letting your hygienist know beforehand allows them to adjust their technique or discuss options like topical numbing gel.

**Do I need X-rays at every cleaning appointment?**
Not necessarily. Bitewing X-rays are typically taken once a year for most adults, though the exact schedule depends on your cavity risk, your age, and what your dentist finds during the exam. Full-mouth X-rays are taken less frequently — often every three to five years for adults with healthy teeth and no active concerns.

**What's the difference between a regular cleaning and a deep cleaning?**
A regular cleaning (prophylaxis) removes plaque and tartar from the visible surfaces of your teeth and just at or slightly below the gumline. A deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) goes further below the gumline to clean the root surfaces of teeth where gum disease has caused the tissue to pull away. Deep cleanings are typically done with local anesthetic and completed in multiple appointments.

Costs vary depending on whether you have dental insurance and what's included in the appointment (such as X-rays). With dental insurance, a routine cleaning and exam is often covered at 100% or subject to a small copay. Without insurance, a cleaning and exam in the Long Beach area typically ranges from around $150 to $350 depending on the practice and services performed.
