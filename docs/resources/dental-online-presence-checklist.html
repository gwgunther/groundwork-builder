The Dental Practice Online Presence Checklist

Download PDF

Basic — do these first

Your website

  • ☐A separate page for each major service — implants, cosmetic, emergency, kids, etc. Not one long “Services” page with everything tucked inside.
  • ☐Homepage, About, and an obvious way to book — a Book Appointment page, contact page, or button on every page.
  • ☐A services page that links to each service — so patients and Google can find every treatment you offer.
  • ☐Pages for nearby towns you actually serve — only if you truly draw patients from those areas.
  • ☐Simple web addresses — like yoursite.com/services/dental-implants/, not yoursite.com/page?id=47.
  • ☐Links from your homepage to your main service pages — not only a booking button.
  • ☐Footer on every page with your name, address, phone, hours, and links to important pages.

Works on phones and loads quickly

  • ☐Padlock in the browser on every page (secure / HTTPS).
  • ☐Looks good on a phone — open your site on your own phone; text readable, buttons tappable, no pinching to zoom.
  • ☐Loads fast on mobile — use Google’s free tool PageSpeed Insights: enter your website, run the test, switch to Mobile, and check that performance is in the green or “good” range. If the main content takes more than a few seconds to appear, patients may leave before it loads.
  • ☐One clear main heading per page — plus subheadings that make sense if someone skims.
  • ☐A unique page title and short description on each page you want on Google (what appears in search results).
  • ☐Descriptions on photos — not filenames like image1.jpg.
  • ☐No broken links — click through after launch and after big updates.

Easy to call or book

  • ☐Phone number on every page — tap-to-call on mobile.
  • ☐A clear “Book” or “Schedule” button visible without scrolling on key pages.
  • ☐Online scheduling works — click through from your site like a patient would (Dentrix, NexHealth, Zocdoc, etc.).
  • ☐Hours and address easy to find — and hours are still correct.

Real photos

  • ☐Your logo on the site.
  • ☐Real photos of your doctor and team — not generic stock smiles.
  • ☐Swap obvious stock photos for your office, team, or before/after shots (with permission).

Google Business Profile (your Google Maps listing)

  • ☐Profile claimed and verified — search your practice on Google Maps; “Claim this business” means it’s not yours yet.
  • ☐Right business categories — e.g. Dentist, plus others that fit.
  • ☐Profile filled out — hours, services, description, website, attributes.
  • ☐Real photos — exterior, waiting room, ops, team.
  • ☐Website link points to your real domain — not an old or duplicate site.
  • ☐Review link saved — from Google → “Ask for reviews”; use at the front desk, in texts, and email signatures.

You own your accounts

  • ☐Domain name registered to you or your practice.
  • ☐Hosting account in your name.
  • ☐Google Business Profile — you’re an owner, not just a manager invited by an agency.
  • ☐Google Analytics — you can log in and see your data.
  • ☐Google Search Console verified for your website (Google’s tool to see how your site appears in search).
  • ☐A copy of your website — backup or files your vendor must give you if you leave.

Your name, address, and phone match everywhere

  • ☐The exact same practice name, address, and phone on your website, Google profile, and directories — down to spelling and suite numbers.
  • ☐Apple Maps — mapsconnect.apple.com
  • ☐Bing Places — bingplaces.com
  • ☐Yelp — biz.yelp.com
  • ☐Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Vitals, WebMD — claim the ones your patients use.

Google can find your pages

  • ☐Sitemap file exists — visit yoursite.com/sitemap.xml or ask your web person; it should list your public pages.
  • ☐Sitemap submitted in Google Search Console — under Sitemaps.
  • ☐Thank-you and staging pages hidden from Google — so only real pages show in search.

Before you call the site “done”

  • ☐Service pages have real content — enough that a patient learns something; mention your city in the headline when it’s true.
  • ☐Doctor bio on About — who you are, training, why patients choose you.
  • ☐FAQ with real questions — cost, pain, recovery, insurance.
  • ☐A few real reviews on the site — with permission.
  • ☐Insurance or financing page — if applicable.
  • ☐Click every page on phone and desktop — test phone, booking, and forms.

Advanced — once the basics are in place

Content that helps you rank

  • ☐Specific, factual writing — what you do, where you are, who the doctors are.
  • ☐FAQ written like patients talk — e.g. “How much do implants cost in [your city]?”
  • ☐Same practice info everywhere — website, Google profile, and directories all agree.
  • ☐Gallery or before/after — only real images with patient consent.

Reviews

  • ☐At least 20 Google reviews (50+ is better); 4.5 stars or higher if you can.
  • ☐A system to ask for reviews — front desk, text, or email.
  • ☐QR code in the office that goes to your Google review link.
  • ☐Reply to every Google review — good and bad.

Deeper website and tracking

  • ☐Structured data set up — code that helps Google understand your address, hours, and services (test here if your web person handles this).
  • ☐Google Analytics tracking phone calls and bookings.
  • ☐Homepage links to your main services — not only “Book now.”
  • ☐Related services linked together — e.g. implants page links to bone grafting.
  • ☐Blog posts link to service pages when relevant.
  • ☐Monthly check of Google Search Console — what people search for and any errors.

Keep it up

  • ☐Post on Google Business Profile — offers, photos, news.
  • ☐Keep collecting reviews.
  • ☐Publish or update content when you add services or want to rank for new topics.
  • ☐Update the site when hours, doctors, insurance, or services change.

Replacing an old website

  • ☐Old web addresses redirect to new ones — your web person sets up redirects so nothing breaks.
  • ☐Save a copy of the old site before canceling a vendor.
  • ☐Update your Google profile’s website link after go-live.
  • ☐Re-verify Google Search Console if your domain changed.
