About Dr. Hoang
Dr. Anthony Hoang
DMD · Spring St. Dentistry, Long Beach

Dr. Anthony Hoang is a Long Beach and Lakewood native who considers it a genuine privilege to practice dentistry in the community where he grew up. After earning his DMD from Tufts University School of Dental Medicine, he completed a General Practice Residency at the Loma Linda VA Medical Center.

What drives Dr. Hoang every day is the human side of dentistry — the relationships he builds with patients and the trust they place in him, visit after visit. He has a special gift for putting nervous patients at ease.

Whether you're coming in for a routine cleaning or a more involved procedure, Dr. Hoang is committed to making sure you feel heard, respected, and well cared for.

Novel components (3)
Staggered Portrait Card Row
Four portrait-orientation cards in a horizontal row with alternating vertical offsets (odd cards ~40px higher). Each: sm corner radius, teal overlay gradient, white serif title top-left, white body text bottom-left. Maps to service/treatment categories.
Oversized Accent Numeral Row
Four stat units on white: oversized teal numeral at ~72–84px in Cormorant Garamond, small dark descriptor label below. No containers, no borders — numbers float on white ground. Preceded by a split two-line heading.
Split Heading with Decorative Toggle Motif
Small inline uppercase "SERVICES" eyebrow + a visual pill toggle element labeled "BALANCE" above the main heading. Below: line 1 near-black serif, line 2 teal serif. Toggle is visual/metaphorical only — not functional.
Fidelity checks (10)
Hero is full-bleed atmospheric poster with md corner radius and thin cursive SVG line-art overlay — removing line-art loses defining character.
Split two-line headings: line 1 in headingDark serif, line 2 in muted teal accent — this color-shift emphasis is the primary typographic identity marker.
Service/treatment cards use portrait orientation with semi-transparent teal overlay — not plain colored cards or icon tiles.
Nav CTA and hero CTA use pill-radius with a bullet dot suffix (e.g. "BOOK A SESSION •") — the dot suffix is a recurring brand punctuation device.
Eyebrow labels are small inline uppercase tracked text, never a badge or pill container. Used once per major section only.
Stat numerals are oversized, in teal accent, and float without surrounding containers or card borders.
Logo uses bullet dot prefix and em-dash separator: • Spring St. — Dentistry format.
Body copy: light weight, small-to-medium size, relaxed line-height. The contrast between large bold serif headings and delicate body text is essential to the airy density.
Single muted teal accent carries all interactive and emphasis work — logo, nav links, CTA fills, split heading line 2, stat numerals. No secondary accent competes.
Page density remains airy — generous vertical padding between sections. Never compress sections to fit more content above the fold.
